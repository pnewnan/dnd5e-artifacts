# The Hollow Road

**Hollowgate → Dunmore** · Levels 1–2 · Foundational road arc

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starting Location** | Hollowgate |
| **Destination** | Dunmore |
| **Core Locations Seen** | Hollowgate · Hollow Road · Fen Farm · Ashfall Watch Blockade · Destroyed Wagon · Corbin Farm · Dunmore |
| **Tone** | Grounded · Cold · Muddy · Uncertain · Human |
| **Primary Threat** | Fear · Greed · Staged beast attacks · Local predation — not supernatural evil |
| **Emotional Core** | The players should help people. That hope matters. |

---

## Arc Overview

The Hollow Road Arc humanizes the world, emotionally attaches the players to ordinary people, establishes political instability, introduces fear and paranoia, and reveals that the road between Hollowgate and Dunmore is collapsing under pressure.

The players are not yet dealing with ancient evils, world-ending threats, or legendary artifacts. They are helping ordinary people survive during a period of growing instability. This emotional grounding is critical: later, wars begin, cities are erased, kingdoms collapse, and buried horrors emerge. The players must first understand what is worth protecting.

The road is failing because convoys have gone missing, patrols are scared, supplies are not arriving, and rumor is doing as much damage as violence. The party should discover that some of the "beast attacks" are staged by ordinary people exploiting the crisis.

This arc is not about monsters on the road.

It is about what people become when they believe no one is coming to help.

The Fen family becomes generous, desperate, and brave.

The Corbin family becomes greedy, violent, and cruel.

Durgan Valeforge becomes cautious and paralyzed.

The party gets to become the reason hope still works.

---

## Themes to Play Up

### Fear spreads faster than truth

Nobody fully understands what is happening along the Hollow Road. Rumors spread constantly: Elkenquayan raiders, bandit attacks, strange creatures, foreign saboteurs, missing patrols, burned farms. The uncertainty itself becomes dangerous. Each rumor generates three more.

> "The road isn't this dangerous because of what is on it. It is this dangerous because everyone believes it is impassable."

### Ordinary people suffer first

Farmers, refugees, exhausted militia, healers, children, struggling settlements. The stakes are personal. Players should repeatedly feel: these are real people, not abstractions or quest-function NPCs.

Linger on the ordinary people long enough that the players remember them. These connections are the emotional architecture that makes later revelations land.

### Small acts matter

Distributing food, healing civilians, rescuing survivors, recovering medicine — these victories matter emotionally. Hope here is not naive. It is a deliberate choice.

> "Because later, hope becomes fragile. Give them something to lose first."

### Civilization is fragile

Roads, supply lines, communication, confidence — once fear spreads, communities isolate, rumors become reality, violence escalates, and ordinary people turn against each other. This is a system failing from the inside: a slow dissolution, not a dramatic collapse.

By the time anyone declares a crisis, it has already been one for months.

---

## Core Story Summary

The party leaves Hollowgate amid reports of missing convoys, burned farms, vanished patrols, and a road that people increasingly believe is cursed, infested, or sabotaged.

They join or accompany a relief effort heading toward Dunmore. Along the way, they encounter **Fen Farm**, where someone is seriously sick and the needed medicine can only be obtained from Dunmore. The family's son, **Tovin Fen**, left toward Dunmore to get medicine and never returned. The family dog, **Barley**, followed him.

Farther along the road, the party reaches a blockade maintained by **Commander Durgan Valeforge**, who refuses to let the relief convoy pass because patrols and wagons keep disappearing. He is not cruel. He is exhausted, frightened, and unwilling to send civilians into an unknown threat.

The party investigates a destroyed wagon and finds claw marks that look wrong: too even, too deliberate, too perfect. Someone is staging beast attacks.

The trail leads to **Corbin Farm**, a seemingly normal but deeply tense homestead. The Corbins are not supernatural villains. They are not professional traffickers. They are greedy farmers who began stealing from damaged wagons, escalated into active robbery, captured witnesses, and now use a chained dire wolf to stage "beast attacks" and disappear evidence.

During dinner, the party hears loud scratching and whining from beneath the farmhouse. The Corbins claim it is their dog. If pressed, they parade out Barley — who is not actually their dog — then put him back in the basement. The meal is drugged.

The party sleeps in the barn loft. In the middle of the night, they wake groggy and possibly exhausted. Someone is stealing their gear below. The dog begins barking from the farmhouse. One of the prisoners has gotten loose. The family panics. The dire wolf starts pulling at its chains in the farmhouse basement.

The party must choose what to do: recover gear, investigate the loft, stop the Corbins, rescue prisoners, deal with the dire wolf, expose the staged attacks, and recover the medicine.

If they rescue Tovin and recover the medicine, he can return to Fen Farm and save his family member.

The party can then return to the blockade with evidence, allowing Durgan to reopen or secure the route to Dunmore.

# Location Structure and Beats

---

## 1. Hollowgate — The Situation

**Purpose:** Establish urgency, instability, and the importance of the road.

Hollowgate is under pressure. Convoys are missing. Food and medicine are delayed. Travelers report attacks, vanished patrols, burned farms, and strange claw marks. Nobody agrees on what is happening.

Some blame Elkenquayan raiders.

Some blame bandits.

Some blame beasts.

Some blame Ashfall Watch or the local fort for failing to respond.

Some are beginning to hoard supplies.

The party should understand that the road to Dunmore matters. It is not just a travel route. It is a supply line.

### Hollowgate / Convoy NPCs

| Name | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Mara Vell** | Relief wagon leader | Middle-aged, practical, exhausted, gruff but kind. Holding things together through sheer willpower. |
| **Edrin Holt** | Militia volunteer, 19 | Clearly inexperienced, trying hard not to look afraid. Let him grow if protected. |
| **Old Thom** | Elderly farmer transporting salvaged tools | Refuses to abandon his tools. Understands the situation perfectly and has decided civilization requires someone to act as though it will continue. |
| **Sella & Brin** | Refugee siblings | Sella holds it together for Brin's sake. Their parents may or may not be found. Do not resolve their story cheaply. |

### What to Play

The relief convoy should feel vulnerable, underprepared, and emotionally real, not heroic.

The players should choose to help because the people are worth helping.

---

## 2. The Hollow Road — Signs of Collapse

**Purpose:** Make the road feel unsafe before combat happens.

As the party travels, they see signs that the region is fraying:

- abandoned carts
- old blood in the mud
- broken wheels
- travelers turning back
- distant smoke
- frightened farmhands
- empty waystations
- half-burned fence lines
- signs warning travelers away
- rumors of burned farms
- rumors of beast attacks

The party does **not** need to directly visit burned farms. Burned farms exist as reports, smoke on the horizon, refugee testimony, and evidence of widening instability. The only victim farm the party directly visits is **Fen Farm**.

### Rumors

Use contradictory rumors.

- "It was wolves. Big ones."
- "No wolf burns a granary."
- "Elkenquayan scouts were seen near the south road."
- "A patrol from Ashfall Watch vanished down there."
- "The claw marks were as long as a man's arm."
- "The farms are doing this to each other now."

### Design Note

The world should feel strained, not destroyed. Yet.

---

## 3. Fen Farm — The Sick Farm

**Purpose:** Humanize the stakes and create the emotional payoff for later.

This is the only victim farm the party directly visits.

Fen Farm is modest and struggling. Someone there is seriously sick or injured, and the needed medicine can only be obtained from Dunmore. The road has become too dangerous for ordinary people, but the family had no other option.

**Tovin Fen** left toward Dunmore to get medicine. He never returned.

**Barley**, the family dog, followed him. Barley also has not returned.

The family is frightened, but decent. They may still share water, shelter, or what little food they have.

### Fen Farm NPCs

| Name | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ilsa Fen** | Sick family member | Mother, grandmother, spouse, or other beloved family member. Needs medicine from Dunmore. |
| **Harlan Fen** | Worried spouse/father | Explains that Tovin went toward Dunmore and Barley followed. Trying to remain composed. |
| **Tovin Fen** | Missing son | Later found as Prisoner Two in the Corbin farmhouse basement. |
| **Barley** | Fen family dog | Followed Tovin and is later used by the Corbins as a cover story. |

### Key Information

The family explains:

- The medicine is in Dunmore.
- The road is blocked or unsafe.
- Tovin went anyway.
- Barley followed him.
- Neither has returned.

They may also mention the apothecary mark:

> "The Dunmore apothecary seals medicine crates with a black briar mark. A briar curled around a little mortar bowl."

### Emotional Function

Fen Farm should make the party care before they reach the bad farm.

Later, when the party finds Tovin, Barley, and the medicine, this scene pays off directly.

---

## 4. Ashfall Watch Blockade

**Purpose:** Show institutional fear, not villainy.

The relief convoy reaches a military blockade on the road. Commander Durgan Valeforge refuses to let civilians and supplies continue because patrols and wagons have vanished.

Durgan is not cruel or stupid. He is exhausted, frightened, and unwilling to send more people to die.

### Ashfall Watch NPCs

| Name | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Commander Durgan Valeforge** | Dwarven militia commander | Rational, disciplined, exhausted. Refuses to let the convoy through because he believes it would be murder without securing the road first. |
| **Berry Underbough** | Young halfling militiaman | Eager, nervous, compassionate. Wants to join the investigation; Durgan refuses because he does not want to send another young soldier to die. |

### Durgan's Position

> "I don't care if it's wolves, raiders, or the Queen's own ghost. Until I know what is taking people off that road, I am not sending civilians through it."

His logic makes sense. This is not a villain encounter. It is a frightened man making a defensible decision.

The right response is to solve the problem he is describing, not overpower him.

### What Durgan Knows

- Several patrols have failed to return.
- A militia wagon was found destroyed.
- Bodies are missing.
- Some reports mention claw marks.
- Some survivors claim beast attacks.
- Others claim people were involved.
- There are reports of burned farms, but no clear pattern.

### Player Objective

Give Durgan proof of what is actually happening.

---

## 5. The Destroyed Wagon — What Does Not Add Up

**Purpose:** Foreshadow Corbin Farm, the dire wolf, and the staged beast attacks.

The party investigates a destroyed militia wagon or convoy cart farther down the road.

They find:

- broken crates
- blood
- drag marks
- claw marks
- missing bodies
- signs of a struggle
- evidence that cargo was removed deliberately
- possibly fragments of apothecary packaging or black wax

At first, it looks like a creature attack.

But on closer inspection, the claw marks are wrong.

### Investigation Detail — The Claw Marks

On a good Investigation check, the marks read as manufactured:

- too even
- too symmetrical
- too consistent
- identical depth and angle
- repeated parallel spacing
- faint metal scoring
- repeated strike patterns

Real animals slip, vary pressure, and tear unpredictably.

These marks were made by a tool.

> "Someone is staging violence."

### Later Payoff

At Corbin Farm, if the party investigates the barn loft, they can find the forged tool used to create these perfect beast marks.

---

## 6. Corbin Farm — The Bad Farm

**Purpose:** Central adventure location, social trap, investigation, combat, rescue, and moral contrast to Fen Farm.

Corbin Farm appears normal from the outside: fenced fields, a farmhouse, a barn, a yard, smoke from the chimney, people trying to look like ordinary farmers.

But the Corbins are on edge.

They are not traffickers.

They are not cultists.

They are not supernatural.

They are greedy farm predators who use a real dire wolf, fake claw tools, and road fear to rob convoys and disappear witnesses.

They began as scavengers.

Then they became thieves.

Then they became ambushers.

Then they became murderers.

Now they have prisoners in the basement, stolen medicine, a chained dire wolf, and a party sleeping in their barn loft.

They are in for a penny, in for a pound.

---

# Corbin Farm Layout

```text
Road
 |
[Farmhouse]
- dinner table
- cellar/basement below
- prisoners hidden below
- Barley held below
- dire wolf chained in reinforced basement/undercroft area

[Yard]
- well
- chopping block
- woodpile
- path between house and barn

[Barn]
- party sleeps in loft
- stolen goods hidden under tarps
- forged beast-mark tool hidden in loft
- party gear may be searched below during the night
```

The dire wolf is in the **farmhouse basement/undercroft**, not the barn. The party is in the barn loft, so the wolf should not be directly beneath them.

The basement is the horror space.

The barn loft is the clue space.

The yard is the danger space between them.

---

# Corbin Farm NPCs

| Name | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Joryn Corbin** | Farm father | Tired, controlling, self-justifying. Started by "taking abandoned goods." Still tells himself he is protecting his family. |
| **Hessa Corbin** | Farm mother | Calm, watchful, and socially dangerous. Notices who eats, who does not, who asks questions, and who looks toward the cellar. |
| **Corren Corbin** | Adult son | Drunk, angry, reckless. Hates pretending. Wants to kill problems quickly. |

| **Nella Corbin** | Younger family member | Conflicted and frightened. Not innocent, but not fully hardened. May freeze, lie, flee, or help depending on party actions. |
| **Prisoner One** | Convoy survivor/witness | Saw enough to identify the Corbins and expose the staged attacks. |
| **Tovin Fen** | Prisoner Two | Missing son from Fen Farm. Tried to get medicine from Dunmore and was captured. |
| **Barley** | Normal dog | Fen family dog. The Corbins use him as cover for basement noises. |
| **Dire Wolf** | Chained beast | Abused animal used as weapon, threat, and evidence-destroyer. Dangerous but not evil. |

# Corbin Family Psychology

The Corbins should not behave like cartoon villains.

They are tense because their crimes have become unmanageable.

They have stolen too much.

They have hurt people.

They have prisoners in the basement.

They have a dangerous animal under the house.

The blockade is nearby.

The party is stronger than ordinary travelers.

One family member drinks too much.

One family member is angry.

One family member wants out.

One family member wants to kill the party immediately.

They are not calm criminals. They are cracking.

### Their Moral Logic

They may say things like:

- "We didn't make the road this way."
- "Those convoys never cared about farms like ours."
- "The goods would've rotted."
- "We took what no one could protect."
- "You think the fort's hands are clean?"
- "We fed you, didn't we?"

# Corbin Farm Beat Order

---

## Beat A — Arrival

The Corbins try to move the party along.

They are polite but tense.

They say the road is dangerous. They say the party should not linger. They claim the attacks happened farther down the road.

If it is near dusk, they reluctantly offer food and a place to sleep in the barn loft.

Possible lines:

- "Road's closed ahead."
- "Nothing here worth your trouble."
- "We already spoke to the militia."
- "Best not linger. Wolves have been bold lately."
- "You look capable. Capable people should know when to leave."

This should feel like hospitality with something sour underneath.

---

## Beat B — Dinner

The party eats with the family.

There should be normalcy: stew, bread, cider, a cramped table, mismatched chairs, family tension.

But the family is on edge.

Corren drinks too much.

Bram mutters things he should not.

Hessa watches who eats.

Joryn controls the conversation.

Nella flinches when noises come from below.

### The Basement Noise

During dinner, the party hears loud scratching and whining from below the farmhouse.

The sound is too loud and too distressed to be easily ignored.

The Corbins say:

> "That's just Barley. Old dog hates being shut up during supper."

If the party presses, they produce Barley.

### The Paraded Dog

One of the Corbins opens the basement door just enough to bring up a normal farm dog.

They block the view down the stairs.

Barley is real.

Barley is scared.

Barley is not their dog.

He belongs to the Fen family.

The Corbins show him, explain the noise away, then put him back downstairs.

### Clues

- Barley does not want to go back down.
- Barley may refuse the stew.
- Barley may go to the kindest party member or the druid.
- The scratches sounded too loud for a normal dog alone.
- The family bolts the cellar afterward.
- A family member reacts too sharply when Barley's name is said.
- If the party remembers Fen Farm, they may realize Barley is the missing dog.

### Dinner Tension Lines

Corren, drunk:

> "Why are we feeding them?"

Hessa:

> "Corren."

Bram, too drunk:

> "Should've put that mutt downstairs with the other one."

Silence.

Hessa:

> "Bram."

---

## Beat C — The Drugged Meal

The meal contains a mild sedative. It is not instant knockout poison and should not remove player agency.

### Farmhouse Draught

Any character who eats a full portion of the meal and later sleeps must make a **DC 12 Constitution saving throw**.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Success | No mechanical penalty, but the character wakes dry-mouthed, nauseated, and aware something was wrong. |
| Failure | The character gains **1 level of exhaustion**. |
| Ate only a little | Roll with advantage. |
| Did not eat | No save. |

Do **not** add the poisoned condition unless you intentionally want the fight to become much harder.

### Rules Note

The **poisoned** condition in 5e does not automatically deal damage. It gives disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks. Poison damage only happens if a specific poison says it deals damage.

For this level 2 scene, exhaustion is enough.

One level of exhaustion gives disadvantage on ability checks, which affects things like Stealth, Perception, Investigation, Athletics, Acrobatics, Insight, and Survival. It makes the scene harder without making combat miserable.

---

## Beat D — Sleeping in the Barn Loft

The family puts the party in the barn loft, not the farmhouse.

This is believable:

- "The house is cramped."
- "The children sleep inside."
- "The loft is warmer than the road."
- "You'll have privacy."
- "The dog snaps at strangers."

In truth, it separates the party from the prisoners in the farmhouse basement.

### The Loft Clue

If the party investigates the loft, they can find the forged tool used to create the staged beast marks.

Possible description:

> Under a folded tarp and a pile of old feed sacks, you find a heavy iron tool with two curved prongs set at a fixed distance. The tips are sharpened and stained dark near the edges. When dragged through wood, it would leave two deep, parallel gouges — exactly like the "claw marks" on the wagon.

This directly connects the investigation scene to the farm.

---

## Beat E — Middle-of-the-Night Inciting Incident

The party wakes before the Corbins finish their plan.

They are groggy. Some may be exhausted.

From the barn loft, they can tell:

- someone is below, stealing their gear
- something is wrong in the farmhouse
- Barley is barking frantically from the house
- voices outside are panicking
- someone mentions the cellar
- one prisoner has gotten loose
- the dire wolf below the house is snarling, slamming, or pulling against chains
- one of the Corbins says something about not letting the party reach the cellar

This is the key moment:

> They are awake.
> The Corbins do not know that yet.
> What do they do?

Do not start in initiative unless they immediately attack. Let the players choose.

### Read-Aloud Option

> You wake in the hayloft with your mouth dry and your limbs heavy. The sleep did not restore you. It dragged you under and left something behind.
>
> Below, in the dark barn, someone is moving through your things.
>
> A buckle snaps. Leather slides over wood. One of your packs opens, and a man whispers, "Take the blades first. Leave the armor."
>
> From the farmhouse, Barley starts barking.
>
> Not whining now. Barking. Frantic, sharp, terrified.
>
> A door slams open. Someone shouts, "Get him back downstairs!"
>
> Then another voice, drunk and furious, bellows from the yard, "We should've done it before supper!"
>
> A woman snaps, "Shut your mouth."
>
> For a moment, everything goes still.
>
> Then, from the farmhouse, you hear a stranger scream.
>
> Below the loft, the figure going through your things freezes.
>
> From somewhere beneath the house, something large pulls against its chain.
>
> The figure below whispers, "They're awake."
>
> Outside, someone says, "Then don't let them reach the cellar."

### What the Party Can Infer

They were drugged.

Their gear is being stolen.

There is a prisoner or victim in the farmhouse.

Barley is connected to the basement.

The family is panicking.

The dire wolf is under the house.

The cellar is the key location.

---

## Beat F — Combat / Chaos

The encounter should have multiple objectives, not just "kill enemies."

### Objective Split

| Objective | Location | Stakes |
|---|---|---|
| Recover gear | Barn floor | Party is weakened and partially disarmed. |
| Investigate the loft tool | Barn loft | Proves staged beast marks. |
| Cross the yard | Between barn and house | Exposed movement under pressure. |
| Rescue prisoners | Farmhouse basement | Human stakes and key testimony. |
| Deal with dire wolf | Farmhouse basement / undercroft | Main physical threat. Dangerous but not evil. |
| Stop evidence destruction | Smokehouse / pantry / yard | Corbins may burn or hide stolen goods. |
| Recover medicine | House storage / pantry / stolen crate | Saves Fen Farm. |

### Level 2 Encounter Guidance

A level 2 party can fight a dire wolf, but do not overload the encounter.

Recommended setup:

| Threat | Use |
|---|---|
| **1 dire wolf** | Main danger. |
| **Joryn or Corren** | Armed human threat; use bandit or thug-lite behavior. |
| **Hessa** | Tries to silence prisoners or destroy evidence. |
| **Bram** | Drunk interference, bell-ringer, or wild card. |
| **Nella** | May flee, freeze, lie, or help depending on party actions. |

Do not have every family member fight optimally.

They are panicking.

The dire wolf is dangerous, but it is not evil. It has been chained, starved, and used.

### Dire Wolf Alternatives

If the dire wolf feels too punishing for a smaller or fragile level 2 party, weaken it slightly:

- reduce HP from 37 to 28–30
- remove Pack Tactics because it is fighting alone
- have it attack whoever hurt or controlled it most recently
- allow Animal Handling, Speak with Animals, food, or broken chains to redirect it

## Beat G — The Farmhouse Basement

In the farmhouse basement, the party finds the truth.

### What Is Below

- **Tovin Fen**, alive but weak
- **Prisoner One**, a convoy survivor or witness
- **Barley**, frantic and protective
- the **dire wolf**, chained in a reinforced section or undercroft
- stolen goods
- stolen medicine
- rope, bloodied cloth, discarded uniforms, or other evidence

### Prisoner One's Testimony

Prisoner One can confirm:

> "It wasn't beasts. Not at first. People stopped the wagon. The wolf came after."

They saw the Corbins attack or stage the aftermath.

They can identify the family.

### Tovin's Testimony

Tovin can confirm:

> "I got the medicine. I was almost home. They took me near the road. The crate is here."

This confirms that Tovin reached Dunmore or a Dunmore medicine source and was captured on the return journey.

---

## Beat H — The Medicine

The medicine should be recoverable but not automatic.

It may be hidden in:

- Hessa's locked pantry cupboard
- a crate in the basement storage area
- a smokehouse cache
- a stolen wagon under a tarp
- a chest of especially valuable goods

### The Apothecary Seal

The crate carries a wax seal.

**Read this exactly as written and nothing more:**

> The crate bears a cracked black wax seal: a thorned briar curling around a mortar and pestle. Beneath it, stamped in small block letters, are the words: DUNMORE APOTHECARY.

> **GM ONLY — do not read this part aloud, and do not have any NPC say it:**
> This is the same mark that hangs above the entrance to The Briar Cup in Dunmore — Ilyra Marr's apothecary, and a Gilded Veil location tied to the Ink Must Not Speak arc. The connection is intentional foreshadowing, not a coincidence. Do not say the word "briar" meaningfully or draw attention to the seal beyond its physical description above. No NPC should say anything like "that's the Briar Cup's mark." If a player has a good eye (or takes notes), let them recognize it themselves when they actually reach The Briar Cup later. **The payoff is in the recognition, not the narration.**

This ties Fen Farm to Dunmore and quietly foreshadows later plant/briar imagery.

---

## Beat I — Resolution at Fen Farm

If the party frees Tovin and recovers the medicine, he can return to Fen Farm.

This is the emotional win.

Maybe the party escorts him.

Maybe they send him with Barley.

Maybe he insists on going immediately.

Maybe they return later and see lanterns lit, Ilsa stabilized, Harlan crying with relief, and Barley curled beside the bed.

This completes the loop:

- The party met suffering people.
- They learned Tovin and Barley were missing.
- They found both alive.
- They recovered the medicine.
- They made a concrete difference.

## 7. Return to Ashfall Watch Blockade

**Purpose:** Convert personal victory into regional progress.

The party can bring evidence back to Commander Durgan:

- stolen convoy goods
- the forged claw-mark tool
- testimony from Prisoner One
- Tovin's rescue
- the recovery of Dunmore medicine
- proof the Corbins staged beast attacks

Durgan can now justify reopening or securing the road.

He should not become triumphant. He should be angry, ashamed, and relieved.

Possible line:

> "I was holding the road against a monster. Turns out the monster had a dinner table."

The relief convoy can proceed toward Dunmore.

## 8. Dunmore Arrival

**Purpose:** End with survival, not victory fireworks.

The convoy reaches Dunmore. Supplies arrive. Some people live because the party acted.

This is meaningful but not clean.

The world is still unstable.

The fort/watch failed.

Rumors will not vanish overnight.

The road is safer, but not healed.

The party should feel:

> "We helped people."

And also:

> "Something is deeply wrong in this kingdom."

# The Real Cause — Revised

The road instability is not one enemy. It is several overlapping problems at once.

## 1. Ordinary Banditry

Criminals are taking advantage of collapsing security, attacking isolated travelers and unprotected refugees. Opportunistic, not organized. They make things worse, but they are not the primary cause.

## 2. Fear and Rumor

Panicked survivors increasingly blame Elkenquay, foreign infiltrators, or monsters. Rumors outpace truth. Dangerous not because of real enemies alone, but because the perception of threat starts justifying military escalation.

## 3. The Corbin Family

The Corbins are the grounded human cause of the immediate Hollow Road crisis.

They have been intercepting supplies, staging beast attacks, stealing goods, capturing witnesses, and using a chained dire wolf to support the lie that something monstrous is haunting the road.

They are not masterminds. They are not a trafficking ring. They are not supernatural agents.

They are local people who discovered they could profit from fear and kept choosing worse.

# What Changed From Earlier Planning

## The unnamed landowner is now the Corbin family

The old concept of an unnamed landowner or hoarder staging creature attacks has been merged into the Corbins. They now fill that role directly. (Originally drafted as "Vale" — renamed to Corbin to avoid confusion with Commander **Durgan Valeforge**, whose surname was too close to work alongside a villain family at the same table.)

## Burned farms are offscreen

There are burned farms in reports, rumors, and background evidence. The party does not need to visit them.

The only farm they directly see as victims is **Fen Farm**.

The only farm they directly investigate as villains is **Corbin Farm**.

## The dire wolf is real, but not the real evil

The arc is still not "about monsters."

The dire wolf exists, but it is an abused weapon and cover story.

The true evil is the family using fear, hospitality, and violence to prey on travelers.

## The poisoned condition is not needed

The drugged meal should create exhaustion, not poison damage.

The **poisoned** condition causes disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks, but it does not deal automatic damage. For level 2, one level of exhaustion is enough pressure.

## Tovin went to Dunmore for medicine

Tovin was not looking for a missing convoy near the farm. He went toward Dunmore to get medicine and was captured on the return journey.

This makes Dunmore important and makes the medicine payoff immediate.

## The apothecary seal is deliberate, silent foreshadowing

The black briar + mortar-and-pestle seal is the same mark as The Briar Cup / Ilyra Marr's apothecary in Dunmore. This is intentional. It must never be spoken aloud or explained by an NPC — describe the seal physically only, and let players make the connection themselves later, at the table, when they actually reach The Briar Cup.

---

# Final Recommended Arc Order

1. **Hollowgate:** rumors, missing convoys, relief convoy forms.
2. **Hollow Road:** signs of collapse, smoke, abandoned carts, rumors of burned farms.
3. **Fen Farm:** sick family, missing Tovin, missing Barley, medicine needed from Dunmore.
4. **Ashfall Watch Blockade:** Durgan refuses passage until the road threat is understood.
5. **Destroyed Wagon:** false claw marks, missing bodies, possible apothecary clue.
6. **Corbin Farm Arrival:** tense hospitality, party encouraged not to investigate.
7. **Dinner:** scratching below, Barley paraded as cover, family tension, drugged meal.
8. **Barn Loft:** party sleeps; loft contains forged beast-mark tool if investigated.
9. **Night Inciting Incident:** party wakes groggy/exhausted while gear is being stolen; Barley barks; prisoner gets loose; family panics.
10. **Farm Encounter:** recover gear, cross yard, rescue prisoners, deal with dire wolf, confront the Corbins.
11. **Medicine Recovery:** black briar apothecary crate found.
12. **Fen Farm Payoff:** Tovin returns with Barley and medicine.
13. **Blockade Resolution:** evidence convinces Durgan to reopen or secure the road.
14. **Dunmore Arrival:** convoy arrives; hope survives, but the kingdom feels fragile.

---

# Emotional Goal — End of Arc

> "We helped people. That hope matters."

By the end of the arc, the players should feel useful, compassionate, protective, emotionally attached to the region, and increasingly worried about the kingdom.

Most importantly: they helped people, the hope is real, the world is worth protecting.

That hope matters because later it becomes much harder to hold onto. Build it here carefully, so that when the campaign later asks the players what they are fighting for, they already know.

---

# Narrative Thread — Dunmore and Dunnshire

Dunmore becomes familiar, human, and emotionally important through this arc.

Much later, when the players discover the erased ruins of Dunnshire, they will subconsciously understand what was lost there too.

The names are not an accident. The parallel is intentional.

Do not underline it.

Let it arrive on its own.
