# Hands That Hold the Line
## What Happened So Far

*Sessions 1–8 · Hollowgate · April–June 2026*

---

## Part One: Three Roads Into Hollowgate

Hollowgate is a border town with a look on its face like it's waiting for something to go wrong. The kind of town where people are polite in the way that means they've learned to be careful. Guards who don't rotate often enough. An innkeeper with very strong opinions about Elkenquay. Fields with coyotes moving closer than they used to.

Three people came into it the same week without knowing about the other two.

---

Vanya came first.

She settled into the Crooked Lantern, listened to the innkeeper's theories about Elkenquay strangling the border towns with new tariffs, and didn't agree or disagree about any of it. She catalogued what she needed to catalogue: patrol patterns, guard numbers, which ones were asleep. The innkeeper had a lot to say about food convoys going missing — six wagons out of Harrowfield, a caravan from the Southern Coast, an iron shipment frozen at the checkpoint — and Vanya let him say it.

She was looking for a scribe named Eric Huwithakay. Find out what he knew. Get whatever he'd collected. Make sure he didn't report any of it. The convoy disappearances were exactly the kind of thing a careful scribe in a border town might have been tracking, which was the problem.

---

Brynn arrived in a hay cart.

More precisely: under the hay. In the hay. Hidden in the hay while the cart rumbled through the gatehouse checkpoint without incident, which was the plan, and it worked, so it was a good plan. Harpies at border crossings generate a certain amount of friction that she'd gotten tired of generating. She surfaced in the stable once the driver was gone, shook herself off, and immediately heard someone in the yard going to absolute pieces at a guard.

A big man. Sun-beaten, bruised across one side of his face, the kind of angry that comes from having had days to get there. He'd been attacked on the road. His whole wagon taken. He'd told people and nobody had done anything fast enough for him, and he was on his second round of telling people, and still nothing.

She didn't catch his name yet. She'd find it later.

---

Aurelia followed the sound of a hammer.

The blacksmith's yard opened out toward a field where a carnival was going up — canvas and rope and a troll hauling timber while workers called to each other over the noise. A tiefling in a dark blue coat patterned with stars was crouched down, entertaining a cluster of children by doing something with his hands that kept making them gasp. Aurelia barely clocked him. Her attention was on the woman directing the whole operation from the center of it.

The woman's name was Chaelynn.

Aurelia knew her from Sarynthia. Knew her face, knew what that face meant, had a reasonably clear picture of what Chaelynn being here probably meant even if she didn't have confirmation yet.

Chaelynn turned, saw her, and didn't stop moving. Didn't stop speaking to the worker with the clipboard. Didn't look back.

Aurelia stood there for a moment, then turned and walked back toward the town.

---

The carnival opened the next evening.

Brynn went because she was curious about what a carnival in a hungry town looked like. (Strained. The performers were doing the work but the food stalls were thin and the mood was that particular kind of festive that takes effort.) A tiefling redirected a man away from a blocked path without breaking stride, and Aurelia appeared at Brynn's elbow.

"That's Magnus Eillin," Aurelia said.

She said it like a fact she was confirming for herself as much as explaining to Brynn. The tiefling in the star coat: older than a story Aurelia knew, moving through the carnival like he'd been doing this exact thing for years, which he had. He mentioned supply issues when Brynn got close enough to talk — the carnival's food situation was the same as the town's. Magnus said it cheerfully, the way a person does when they've been saying it to people who can't fix it and have made peace with that.

Vanya came to the carnival looking for a different kind of information, spent part of it buying snares and barbed wire from the trading post, and found the two of them on the way back.

They walked to the mill.

---

At the gatehouse, on the way, Aurelia and Vanya stopped to ask about the scribe.

The guard's name was Jeffrey. He had the look of a man who'd answered this question twice already this week and wasn't sure why it kept coming up. Eric Huwithakay had been missing for two weeks. His belongings were still at his house — C6 on the town map. Nobody knew where he'd gone.

Two weeks. Vanya filed that away. Aurelia thanked Jeffrey and kept her face very still. They were both looking for the same man for very different reasons, and neither of them said that out loud.

---

In the mill loft with the doors shut, Brynn let her coat fall open and showed them her wings.

She'd been keeping them hidden. She'd hidden them at the border crossing, hidden them from the stable where she'd made her nest for the night, tucked them down in crowds. Standing in the mill loft with the carnival noise filtering through the wall, she just let them out.

Vanya looked at them and then looked back at Brynn's face.

"Okay," she said.

That was more or less the entire conversation about it. They agreed to work together. None of them said everything. That was fine.

---

## Part Two: The Coyote Incident

Vanya had a contract with a town contact that required her to show physical evidence of work done at the tree line — a coyote problem, specifically, the kind of problem that had been pushing into the fields. She arranged to go with Jeffrey, framed it correctly, and the group came along: herself, Brynn, and Aurelia with a bag of food for the road.

The coyote appeared at the tree line the way coyotes do when they've gotten too comfortable — didn't run, just watched. Brynn, whose instincts are shaped by years of hunting in which hesitation has costs, lunged.

Her foot caught something. She went down. The coat opened.

The coyote, to be fair, had not expected this either.

Vanya put an arrow in it on the second shot. They carried it back.

---

At the mill, Brynn propped the carcass against the wall and looked at the other two.

She said: I'm a harpy. The wings earlier weren't an accident, they were just earlier than she'd planned. She was traveling because she was looking for something. She carried a staff with a hollow sphere at the top that read constellations and she'd had it long enough that it was part of how she moved. She said most of this plainly, without making a production of it.

Aurelia said that she had come to Hollowgate for Eric Huwithakay.

She didn't say more than that yet. But she said it, which was something.

They agreed to go to the carnival that evening together.

---

## Part Three: What Eric Left

Session four, and Vanya had been thinking about the papers she'd seen Aurelia carrying.

Supply disruption notes. Someone tracking convoy movement: which wagons disappeared, from where, on what schedule. The notes weren't records of theft. They were investigation. Whoever wrote them had been trying to understand the pattern.

Vanya took the papers from Aurelia's cloak. Not gently, but not harshly either — more the way you pick something up off a table when you need to read it. She read them, handed them back.

"He wasn't stealing them," Aurelia said. "He was watching."

"Then we should find the rest of his notes," Vanya said.

---

The carnival gave them cover. Music from the far end of town, voices, the kind of noise that makes a broken window less of an event. Vanya checked the door for traps, found none, couldn't get it open, and Brynn went through the window.

Eric's house had been searched before them.

Not carelessly — carefully, by someone who'd had a reason to be careful. The drawers were mostly right. The cabinet was at a small angle. They would have missed it if they hadn't been looking for it. The burn tray on the desk had a full bed of ash in it, correspondence that had been destroyed before whoever came last had left.

The house had a hidden correspondence slot in the wall — a channel back to Sarynthia. A go-bag under the floorboards, packed, untouched. A Date-Based Cipher Key that could decode Eric's official scribe entries into something else. A board in the back room with convoy routes cross-referenced against disappearances, because Eric had been keeping track the same way Aurelia had seen in the papers.

Eric had packed a bag and never come back for it.

---

While they were still inside, Brynn spotted two workers heading for the door from the window.

She answered it.

"There's a poisoned princess inside," she said. Her face was completely neutral.

The workers stood in the doorway.

"Contagious," she added.

The workers, apparently deciding this was plausible enough to accept, explained their actual concern: the carnival was running low on food, the mayor had put up a reward for recovering missing convoy contents, and could anyone in there help. They went back to the carnival grounds. The door closed.

Magnus had been one of the workers. He'd exited through the window rather than be questioned about what he was doing there. He did it with the energy of someone who had committed fully to the bit and was not going to acknowledge what had just happened.

Aurelia watched him go.

---

## Part Four: McCoy

The angry man from the stable had a name: McCoy. He was staying upstairs at the inn-tavern, and Brynn rented the room next door and knocked.

He didn't need much encouragement to talk. The story had been pressing on him for days looking for somewhere to go. His whole wagon, ten miles west of town — not bandits, kobolds, small figures with spears that moved fast and knew exactly what they were doing. He showed the bruising. Brynn gave him one of the berries from her belt.

Every missing wagon had the same signature. Kobolds working the west road. Whatever was moving them had apparently moved them recently, because this hadn't been a problem before this season.

They went to the mayor.

---

The mayor's office was a room that had lost a fight with paper. Stacked reports, ledgers, folders on top of folders, a desk that had surrendered to the concept of surface area sometime in the previous year. The mayor herself had the energy of someone who had been the most competent person in every room she'd been in for twenty years and was tired of it.

She offered four hundred gold for the recovery of the wagon contents. She confirmed the food shortages. She corrected one detail in McCoy's timeline — a small discrepancy that didn't resolve cleanly — and Brynn noted it and didn't push on it yet.

Four hundred gold.

They didn't tell her about the kobolds. The bounty was theirs to claim.

---

## Part Five: The Forest Guardian

West road, then off it, into the trees. The forest floor was quiet in the particular way of a forest that has something paying attention through it. Mycelial networks running under their feet, connecting everything to everything else, and somewhere in that network, something was following their path.

She stepped out from between two oaks.

Eight feet tall. Antler-like branches where hair might have been. Bark-thick skin, robes the color of the forest floor in autumn, a staff that looked like it had grown rather than been cut. She moved with the certainty of something that has never been out of place anywhere in a forest.

Her name was Hypha of Chronewood.

She was looking for a Heart Seed — a sacred, rune-marked acorn that was necessary for the next growth cycle of the Cronewood. Kobolds had taken it. They had, apparently, believed it to be a dragon egg. Hypha delivered this information with the patient resignation of someone who has accepted that the world is full of creatures making understandable mistakes about things that happen to be extremely important.

She had tracked them through the mycelial network. She knew where they were. She was going with the party.

This was not offered as a question.

---

They found the camp before the camp found them. Two wagons in a clearing — McCoy's was one of them, identifiable by the paint on the axle — with kobold guards positioned with the confidence of creatures who have never been ambushed.

Vanya shot first.

Hypha came out of the tree line behind the arrow. Her thorn whip cracked and took the second guard off its feet. Aurelia fired from prone under the wagon, which is a decision that requires a certain amount of commitment to the moment. The last guard threw its spear down.

A second kobold appeared from the front of the forward wagon, empty-handed, looking at the scene with the expression of someone who had walked into the wrong situation through no fault of their own.

"I appraised the goods," it said immediately. "I sold things. I didn't know they were stolen."

Its name was Robyn. It was, apparently, the appraisal kobold.

---

## Part Six: Grubb's Warren

Hypha asked where the Heart Seed was. She asked the wounded kobold, who was currently being kept from going anywhere by Brynn's weight on its chest. The kobold was not especially forthcoming. Hypha picked it up and put it against the cave wall. The kobold decided to be more forthcoming.

Chieftain. Deeper in. Cave system north of the clearing.

Vanya picked up Robyn by the scruff and pointed him at the cave entrance. Robyn began walking.

---

Inside: a sleeping guard at a side table, a map on that table that Brynn lifted clean without breaking stride. The map was partial but useful — tunnel layout, marked traps, a notation that said *Cheese Trap* with what appeared to be genuine enthusiasm about the naming, and a pit. Hypha enlarged the guard's lantern until it lit the whole passage. The guard woke up, saw Hypha, and decided that the chieftain was definitely deeper in and they were absolutely welcome to continue.

They navigated the Cheese Trap. The pit required attention. The Cheese Trap was everything the name promised and Brynn took a moment to appreciate it.

The chieftain's pet was in an alcove near the back — large, curious, watching them with the mild interest of an animal that has decided they weren't worth immediate action. Robyn said not to provoke it and they didn't.

---

Chieftain Grubb.

She was covered in jewelry from shoulder to wrist, layers of it, stolen from everywhere and worn like a statement. Fourteen kobolds flanked her in two rows. The cave smelled of torch smoke and confidence.

She wasn't aggressive. She was negotiating.

She didn't have the Heart Seed. She'd sold it. The trade had not gone entirely as she'd planned, and to demonstrate what happened to trades that went badly, she had a kobold wheel out a corpse. The corpse had been a messenger once. It had been mostly eaten.

"We could do a different kind of trade," Grubb said.

She was watching them the way someone watches people when they want to know which of them makes the decisions.

Hypha looked at the corpse for a long moment.

"Tell me who you sold it to," she said.

Grubb smiled with all her teeth.

The session ended there.

---

## Part Seven: The Bottom of a Well

They were in the warren before the negotiations finished.

Grubb admitted it: she'd sold the Heart Seed to a buyer, and she could take them to the buyer, but she wanted entertainment first — a contest of strength, because that's the kind of thing that passes for hospitality in a kobold chieftainship. Her pet was between them and the door. Three kobolds to the sides, six more behind.

Before the contest could begin, Hypha cast a spell on the pet.

The pet looked at Hypha. Something shifted in its eyes — the particular settling of a large animal that has just decided that someone is on its side. It turned to face Grubb.

Vanya shot the chieftain.

This was the end of the negotiation phase.

---

The chaos that followed was comprehensive. Hypha's thorn whip took a kobold off its feet. Brynn raised her hands and cast Thunder Wave, which is fifteen feet of compressed sound in an enclosed cave, and the kobolds who caught the edge of it went into the walls. Grubb took the arrow and came for Vanya with the single-minded fury of a creature that has been shot by someone it was doing business with.

She was strong. Much stronger than Vanya had accounted for. Grubb had her pinned in about four seconds, which is a faster result than the size differential would suggest and speaks well of the chieftain's fundamentals as a wrestler.

Vanya looked up from the floor.

"Are you entertained?" she asked.

Grubb looked at her. The pet was currently doing its best to express its new loyalties through Grubb's armor. The rest of the kobolds were in various states of reconsidering their career choices.

"For that," Grubb said, "I will let you live."

She set Vanya down and walked away.

---

Robyn was calling from a side passage. He'd found something.

The tunnel narrowed. The torchlight thinned out. A well — old stonework, older than the kobolds in it, probably older than the town above it. A ladder going down.

Vanya went first.

The chamber at the bottom was small, silty, cold. Still water smell and something else under it. The man in the blue tunic had been there for some days. Curled slightly to the left, the way bodies settle when no one has arranged them. A stab wound below the sternum. The kind of placement you learn.

Aurelia came down the ladder. She looked at the tunic first. Then the face.

She stood there.

"Eric," she said. "Eric Huwithakay."

The go-bag was still packed in the house upstairs. He'd been ready to run. He hadn't made it.

They looked up. Above the chamber, a ladder, rising to a stable at ground level. The same ordinary afternoon light as everywhere else, with no awareness of what was below it.

The Heart Seed was still out there. The buyer was still unknown. The kobolds had been paid to take the convoys — someone had wanted the roads disrupted, and someone had pointed kobolds at them to make it happen.

They climbed out.
